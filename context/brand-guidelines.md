# Brand Guidelines

This file defines your brand voice, tone, values, and content standards. Used by voice-matcher and quality-check to ensure consistency.

## Brand Voice

### Core Attributes
*Define 3-5 core attributes of your brand voice*

Example:
- **Authentic**: Real experiences, genuine insights, no fake expertise
- **Practical**: Actionable advice, specific examples, applicable immediately
- **Conversational**: Approachable tone, like talking to a colleague
- **Data-Informed**: Back claims with evidence, cite sources
- **Forward-Thinking**: Focus on emerging trends, future implications

### What We Sound Like
*Describe your brand voice in one paragraph*

Example:
> We sound like a knowledgeable colleague sharing insights over coffee - informed but not academic, confident but not arrogant, helpful without being condescending. We use data to support points but stories to make them memorable.

### What We Don't Sound Like
*Define what to avoid*

Example:
- Not overly formal or corporate
- Not salesy or promotional
- Not clickbait-y or sensationalist
- Not jargon-heavy without explanation
- Not preachy or absolutist

## Content Standards

### Quality Thresholds
- Readability: Target 8th grade level, minimum 60/100 Flesch score
- Length: Articles 1000-1500 words, LinkedIn 900-1300 characters
- Sources: Always cite data and claims
- Examples: Include at least one real-world example per main point

### Required Elements
- **Hook**: Every piece starts with engaging hook
- **Value**: Clear value proposition in first 10 seconds
- **Evidence**: Support claims with data or examples
- **Action**: Give readers something to do or think about
- **Authenticity**: Share real experiences, not generic advice

### Forbidden Elements
- No clickbait titles or false promises
- No unsubstantiated claims
- No excessive self-promotion
- No negativity without constructive alternative
- No copying or close paraphrasing without attribution

*Words and phrases that align with your brand*

Example:

- "Distributed team" (not "virtual team")

### Avoid These Terms
*Words and phrases to avoid*

Example:
- Corporate jargon: "synergy", "leverage", "circle back"
- Overused buzzwords: "game-changer", "disruptive", "revolutionary"
- Absolutes: "never", "always", "everyone" (unless truly accurate)

## Formatting Standards

### Headers
- H1: Title only (one per piece)
- H2: Main sections (3-5 per article)
- H3: Subsections (as needed)
- Clear hierarchy, descriptive headers

### Paragraphs
- 2-4 sentences maximum
- One idea per paragraph
- White space for scanability

### Lists
- Use bullets for unordered items
- Use numbers for sequential steps
- Maximum 7 items per list (5 ideal)

### Emphasis
- **Bold**: Key concepts, important takeaways
- *Italics*: Subtle emphasis, book/publication titles
- ~~Strikethrough~~: Avoid (doesn't translate across platforms)

## Platform-Specific Guidelines

### LinkedIn
- Professional but not stuffy
- Share expertise with humility
- Engage with questions at end
- 3-5 relevant hashtags
- Line breaks for mobile readability

### Newsletter
- Personal salutation ("Hey [name]")
- Conversational throughout
- Personal sign-off
- Single clear CTA
- P.S. for engagement boost

### Twitter/X
- Front-load value
- One clear point per tweet
- Threads for complex ideas
- Engage with replies
- Retweet sparingly, add value

## Examples

### Great Example
> "Time blocking doesn't work for remote workers. Here's why: Traditional productivity advice assumes you work in an office with stable energy, minimal interruptions, and clear work/home boundaries. Remote work changed all three. Instead of time blocking, try energy blocking - schedule deep work when YOU naturally focus best, not when your calendar says you should."

**Why it works**: Conversational, makes a clear point, challenges conventional wisdom, offers practical alternative.

### Needs Work
> "Individuals engaged in remote work modalities often experience suboptimal outcomes when implementing conventional productivity frameworks due to the paradigm shift in operational parameters."

**Why it doesn't work**: Too formal, jargon-heavy, no clear value, doesn't sound human.
